**The big picture:**
CsoS2 largely, but especially the C-terminal region, is disoredered when free but adopts a well defined conformation when bound to shell proteins (CsoS1A hexamers) The repeated [IV]TG motifs act as the points of contact/ hooks for these two proteins. 

**The question:**
Do free-state ensembles (ML-generated, coarse-grained and my own atomistic MD if I can) sample the bound conformation of the motifs, and how much do methods differ?

**Goals:**
compare methods to eachother (bound-likeness) quantify uncertainty and convergence of different computationally derived theoretical free-state conformations. Check whether simulations keep the experimental bound pose stable

**Limitations:**
I have NO experimental data. I will not be able to say which method gives the correct free-state ensemble. Over the course of this project I am gonna be like a scavenger scraping together what other people have already done and seeing if I can draw some interesting conclusions from it for my own personal enrichment. 

**Original motivation:**
I was curious about the CsoS2 conformations but its disordered regions are a poor fit for software like AlphaFold, which predicts only a single structure rather than an ensemble. I came across Head-Gordon lab's work on IDP ensembles, including IDPForge and AlphaFlex. This project arose as a result of wanting to familiarise myself with these resources while also answering my intial questions about CsoS2 using ensembles already available in the Protein Ensemble Database, and hopefully my own simulations if I get that far.
